Kneser-Ney smoothing

Problem it solves:
23% of the vocabulary in my corpus is hapax (only occurs once) and held-out responses are full of words in unseen contexts. Maximum Likelihood Estimation gives these words zero probability (the coverage cliff). Smoothing redistributes some probability mass from seen events to unseen ones.

There are more naive ways to do smoothing such as Add-One (Laplace). This just adds 1 to every count. But this would drown real signal in a sparse corpus such as mine. Add-k is a similar technique with a smaller constant. These both treat unseen events as equally likely, which is wrong.

KN is special because:
1. Absolute discounting. Instead of adding mass, KN subtracts a small fixed amount (a discount- D, typically 0.75) from every observed count. This pools the freed-up mass to distribute among unseen events. It is gentler to subtract from seen events that add to unseen.
2. Continuation probability - the real KN insight. When KN falls back to a lower order for an unseen event, it doesn't ask how frequent is this word, it asks in how many different contexts doe this word appear? E.g. the word 'Fransisco' is fairly frequent, but it appears in essentially one context - after 'San'. So when guessing the next word in a novel context, 'Francisco' should get low probability despite its frequency, because it never appears otherwise. A word like 'the' follows almost everything. A continuation count - the number of distinct contexts a word completes is used rather than raw frequency. KN models versatility not just frequency.

Specifically in my corpus, the word 'you' follows '<SEP>' a lot, therefore it would have a high continuation count in KN. However, a word like 'gravity' appears in exactly one context, therefore a low continuation count. KN keeps it low, whereas general smoothing would give it some mass.

KN's continuation-count mechanism would distinguish my versatile frame-words (you, it, the) from my single-context content-words (gravity, Tuesday), assigning fallback probability by contextual versatility rather than raw frequency — precisely the distinction my per-category hapax analysis surfaced.

I identified Kneser-Ney smoothing as the principled solution to the rare-word/OOV problem quantified in §[hapax].
